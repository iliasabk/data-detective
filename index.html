Data Detective — can you trust this claim?

A 10–15 minute investigation for readers around 15. Three short cases: a dramatic chart, a suspicious survey, and a headline that confuses patterns with causes. All people, places and datasets are fictional.

Briefing

You are the fact-checker for the fictional Harbourview Gazette. Three drafts just landed on your desk. Each hides a different way that honest-looking data can mislead:

  • Case 1 — a chart whose axis makes a small difference look enormous;
  • Case 2 — a survey that asked the wrong people;
  • Case 3 — two numbers that rise together, but for a hidden reason.

In each case you will manipulate the evidence yourself, then write one careful sentence of your own. Nothing here asks you to distrust all data — only to look twice.

Takes about 10–15 minutes. Works with keyboard alone: use Tab to move and Enter to choose. Your progress is kept only in this page — no account, nothing sent anywhere.

Carry this forward

Next time a chart shocks you, ask three questions: Where does the axis start? Who was asked? What else moves with both? For practice, find one chart in this week's news and check its axis range — most honest charts start at zero for bars.
